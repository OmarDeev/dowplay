Grid Manager is a free renewable energy game. Students keep a town powered every hour of
the day using solar farms, wind turbines, batteries, pumped hydro and, on later levels, gas
plants. A chart under the map shows hour by hour where the town's power comes from, what is
charging the batteries, what is wasted, and where the blackouts are. The goal is to keep the
lights on for as little money and CO₂ as possible.

## How to play

Choose a machine and tap squares on the map to build it. Each square shows how much sun or
wind it gets: hilltops are windier, valleys are shaded. Move along the chart to see any hour
on the map, with the sun, the turbines, the battery levels and the houses' lights. Then
press **Run the day** to play it through. If there's a blackout, the game explains why: not
enough energy stored, or batteries that can't deliver it fast enough.

Each level earns up to three stars: one for keeping the lights on (and under the CO₂
limit), two for staying within the budget, and three for matching the cheapest design we
know.

## The levels

| # | Level | Years | Big idea |
|---|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Sunny School | 9 | Supply must meet demand every hour |
| 2 | After Dark | 9 | Storage moves energy from day to night; MW vs MWh |
| 3 | Windy Hill | 9–10 | Mixing sources; where you build matters |
| 4 | Evening Rush | 10 | Battery power (MW) vs energy (MWh) |
| 5 | Grey Skies | 10–11 | Designing for the worst day |
| 6 | Backup Plan | 11–12 | Gas backup, CO₂ limits, cost vs emissions |
| 7 | Smart Timing | 12 | Moving flexible demand to sunny hours |
| 8 | Still Week | 12–13 | Long-duration storage for a still, grey spell |

## Tips for teachers

- **Year 9:** After Dark makes the link between power and energy concrete. Ask how many
  megawatt-hours the town uses at night before students build any batteries.
- **Years 10–11:** Evening Rush and Grey Skies show why a grid needs more than average
  output. Ask students to explain a blackout from the chart.
- **Years 12–13:** Backup Plan, Smart Timing and Still Week are real energy-policy
  trade-offs: cost against emissions, storage against demand-side response. They work well
  for discussion and extended writing.

Every level ends with a short "What you learned" summary you can use as a plenary.
