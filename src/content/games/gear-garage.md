Gear Garage is a free gears game. Students place gears on a pegboard to connect a motor to a
machine (a winch, a turntable, a lift, a garage door) and make it turn at the right speed,
in the right direction and with enough force. The gears behave like real ones: they mesh,
jam, and stall when the load is too heavy for the motor.

## How to play

Pick a gear size, then tap a hole on the pegboard to place it. Gears that touch mesh
automatically. Each machine shows the speed and direction it needs, and on later levels the
torque too. Press **Check my gears** to see if it works. Tick **Show the numbers** to see
the speed and torque of every gear.

Each level earns up to three stars: one for a machine that works, two for staying within the
budget, and three for matching the cheapest design we know.

## The levels

| # | Level | Years | Big idea |
|---|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | First Gear | 8 | Meshing gears turn each other, in opposite directions |
| 2 | Same Way Round | 8 | Idler gears change direction |
| 3 | Need for Speed | 8–9 | Gear ratio = driver teeth ÷ driven teeth |
| 4 | Heavy Lifting | 9 | Trading speed for torque |
| 5 | Round the Engine | 9–10 | Idlers don't change the overall ratio |
| 6 | Compound Gears | 10–11 | Ratios multiply in a compound train |
| 7 | Garage Door | 11–12 | Rack and pinion, v = ωr |
| 8 | Every Mesh Counts | 12–13 | Efficiency multiplies, P = Tω |

## Tips for teachers

- **Years 8–9:** ask students to predict which way the last gear will turn before they
  place it. Counting the gears in a row is the whole trick.
- **Years 9–10:** in Heavy Lifting, students discover that a slower output is a stronger
  one. Link it to levers and moments.
- **Years 11–13:** Garage Door and Every Mesh Counts need a calculation first. Ask for the
  gear ratio, speed or efficiency on paper, then build it to check.

Every level ends with a short "What you learned" summary you can use as a plenary.
