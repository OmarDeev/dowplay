Light It Up is a free circuit-building game. Students wire up the rooms of a house so the
lamps, doorbell and heater all work, and try to do it within a budget. Behind the scenes the
game solves every circuit the way an electrical engineer would, so the voltages and currents
are real. Put two lamps in series and they really do glow dimmer. Draw too much current and
the fuse really does blow.

## How to play

Choose a part from the toolbar, then drag along the dotted grid to lay it: wire at first,
then resistors and thick cable on later levels. Connect every lamp and appliance to the
battery, then press **Check my circuit**. Tick **Show the numbers** to see the voltage and
current at every part, and watch the current flow around the wires.

Each level earns up to three stars: one for a circuit that works, two for staying within the
budget, and three for matching the cheapest design we know.

| 8 | Power to the Shed | 12–13 | Cable resistance and losses |

## Tips for teachers

- **Years 8–9:** before anyone presses Check, ask the class to predict which lamp will be
  brighter, and why. Levels 1–4 make series and parallel easy to see.
- **Years 10–11:** in levels 5 and 6, ask students to work out the resistor or the fuse
  current on paper first, then build it and check with Show the numbers.
- **Years 12–13:** Smart Doorbell shows why a potential divider's output drops when you
  connect a load, and Power to the Shed shows why long cables waste power.

Every level ends with a short "What you learned" summary you can use as a plenary.
